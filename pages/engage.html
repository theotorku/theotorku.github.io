Different conversations require different contexts. Choose the path that fits your situation. I read every message, but respond selectively to ensure conversations start at a strategic level.

For Recruiters

Hiring for a Role

I'm selectively open to senior technical roles where I can have outsized impact. Not interested in lateral moves or title inflation.

Good Fit

  • Principal/Staff engineer with product ownership
  • Technical co-founder or founding engineer
  • Head of Engineering at sub-50 person company
  • Technical advisor roles (fractional)

Not a Fit

  • Feature factory environments
  • Roles without architectural influence
  • Companies without clear technical vision
  • Remote-hostile cultures

What to Include

  • Company stage and funding (if applicable)
  • The hardest technical problem you're solving
  • Why you think I'd be specifically good for this
  • Compensation range (saves us both time)

For Founders

Building Together

Open to co-founder conversations, technical advisory, and contract engagements for early-stage companies with ambitious technical problems.

What I Do

  • Zero-to-one technical architecture
  • AI/ML system design and implementation
  • Technical due diligence (sell-side)
  • Fractional CTO for pre-Series A

What I Don't Do

  • Staff augmentation
  • Maintenance of existing systems
  • Projects without clear decision-maker
  • Equity-only compensation (negotiable for co-founder)

What to Include

  • One-paragraph problem statement
  • Current stage (idea, prototype, revenue)
  • Your background and why you're building this
  • What you need and timeline

Other Inquiries

Something Else?

If your inquiry doesn't fit the categories above, you can still reach out. I'm particularly interested in conversations about systems thinking, technical writing, and unconventional collaboration formats.

I don't do: speaking engagements, podcast appearances, or "picking your brain" calls. Not out of arrogance—just limited bandwidth. If we haven't met, a written exchange is the best first step.

What to Expect

Response Time

I batch email twice daily. Expect a response within 48 hours if your message is clear and relevant. No response after a week means it's not a fit—no offense intended.

First Conversation

If we move forward, the first call is typically 30 minutes. I'll have read everything you sent and will come with specific questions. I expect the same preparation in return.

Decision Speed

I make decisions quickly when I have sufficient information. If I ask follow-up questions, it's because I'm seriously considering it. I'll never leave you hanging.
